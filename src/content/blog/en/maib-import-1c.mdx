If you keep your books in 1C and get a monthly account statement from
**Moldova Agroindbank (maib)** as a PDF, you know the problem: the data can't
be imported directly — it has to be typed in by hand, row by row, into the
bank documents journal. For a statement with dozens of transactions, that
means hours of work and the risk of a typo in an amount.

**bank-converter** solves exactly that step: upload the PDF, check the
transactions in an editable table, and export straight into the format 1C
understands natively — **1CClientBankExchange**.

## What you'll need

- The account statement as a PDF, downloaded from maib internet banking
- A free bank-converter account (2 statements per month included)
- Access to the 1C database where you track payments

## Step 1 — Download the statement from maib internet banking

From your maib internet banking account, go to the statements section and
download the period you need as a **PDF**. bank-converter reads the text
straight out of the PDF — no special export or scanning required.

## Step 2 — Upload the PDF to bank-converter

Open the [converter](/converter) and drop the PDF file into the upload area
(or tap to pick it manually). Within seconds, the app automatically extracts
each transaction's date, description, and amount.

## Step 3 — Check and fix the transactions

Automatic recognition is fast, but it's worth a check — especially if the
statement has unusually formatted entries. The preview table is fully
editable: you can fix any cell (date, description, amount, currency) right
on the page before exporting.

## Step 4 — Export as 1C (TXT)

Click **Export 1C (TXT)**. The resulting file follows the
1CClientBankExchange 1.02 format, with a `СекцияДокумент=Платежное
поручение` section for every transaction and **windows-1251** encoding —
exactly what 1C expects on import.

## Step 5 — Import the file into 1C

In 1C, open the bank documents journal and use the **"Загрузить" / "Load
from exchange file"** function, then pick the downloaded TXT file. The
payment documents show up automatically, amounts and descriptions already
filled in — all that's left is to confirm and post them to the right
accounts.

## Why not just export XLSX or CSV?

We do offer those too — bank-converter actually exports all three formats:
**XLSX** (via exceljs, with formatted columns and amounts as real numbers),
**CSV** (with a BOM, so Excel picks up diacritics correctly), and **1C
TXT**. The difference is that the 1C file is ready to import straight into
your accounting software, with no intermediate conversion step.
